# Pane-capture concept matrix — Plan 009 v0.1

| Concept | Positive escape barrier | Thickness strategy | Principal advantages | Principal risks | Current disposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| End-loaded continuous rails, printed end gate, transverse 1.75 mm filament pin | Rails block upward escape; gate and pin block axial escape | Common tall channel with optional backing spacers | Clear load path, inexpensive coupon, reuses known pin stock, pane never flexed during installation | First mechanics coupon exceeds 80 mm depth; removable pin needs containment; gate clearance is tight | **Print first** |
| End-loaded continuous rails with keyed flexible end clip | Rails block upward escape; hooked clip shoulders block axial escape | Common channel with optional spacer | No loose filament pin and potentially more compact | Clip fatigue, print-direction weakness, and impact could release the same flexible element that provides retention | Retain as comparison after rail-fit results |
| End-loaded continuous rails with an integral, manually actuated compliant end latch | Rails block upward escape; a sprung shoulder rises behind the pane end to block axial escape | Latch blocks the pane end independent of pane thickness; common channel may still use a replaceable spacer | No loose gate or pin, fewer assembly steps, and potentially fits within the 80 mm envelope | Fatigue, creep, root fracture, accidental actuation, and print-direction weakness; glass must not be used to force the latch aside | **Leading v0.3 comparison after user suggestion and v0.2 gate-frame failure** |
| Underside-loaded pane with full sliding bezel and transverse lock | Lid shoulder blocks upward escape; captured bezel blocks downward escape; cross-lock prevents bezel translation | Replaceable bezel/spacer variants below the pane | Closest to the existing lid architecture and fully covers edges | Large sliding part, debris/friction over a long travel, may reduce visible area or load glass if warped | Retain as primary alternative for coupon comparison |
| Rigid bezel secured by metal screws | Screw heads and bezel provide positive capture | Spacers or compliant washers | Strong, compact, well-understood retention | Added hardware, tools near glass, thread wear, loose parts, and possible stacking interference | Do not prototype unless printed positive concepts fail |

The first coupon round tests the common rail cross-section, pane-thickness
ladder, pin-bore ladder, and pinned-gate load path. It does not select a final
concept. At least the sliding-bezel or keyed-clip alternative must receive a
focused physical comparison before a production lid is chosen.

Following the v0.2 test, the integral compliant latch is preferred over a loose
flexible clip for the next comparison. It must be intentionally depressed by a
finger-access feature during installation and removal; the glass must slide
without camming, forcing, or prying the latch. Its relaxed shoulder must provide
a positive geometric stop rather than relying on friction. Coupon testing must
include repeated cycles, creep observation, impact orientation, and accidental-
release checks.
